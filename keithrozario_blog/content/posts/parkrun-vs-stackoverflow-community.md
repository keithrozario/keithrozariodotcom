I occasionally run parkruns at a park near my home. It's a nice feeling getting up before anyone else, and dragging myself to the park at 7 in the morning, only to subject myself to a hard 5km run in hot and humid Singapore. 5km isn't a lot of most runners, but I'm a poor runner and it requires and all out effort from me just to finishunder 30 minutes.

Park run is a volunteer run event, where everyone at the event is either a participant or a volunteer, no one is paid to organize the event and there are no sponsors -- and it's AWESOME. In most parks there is a strong cohort of regulars that you sometimes mingle with before, during and after the runs, and there's usually a large contigent of first timers as well. 

The event starts with a first-timer briefing, usually 5 minutes long explaining to folks the course that the run will take, some safety and security tips and answering any questions the participants might have. Then there is the full run briefing, where the run director gives an event specific briefing. Volunteers are introduced, first-timers are welcomed with cheers, and milestones (like someone completing their 50th parkrun) are celebrated with photos. 

Then everyone makes their way to the starting line, and the event is officially kicked off with the run director flagging everyone off with 2 timekeepers by their side, the runners then start running (or walking), and the volunteers then begin working on setting up the finishing line. During the race, volunteer pace-setters wearing their pace numbers help you maintain your desired pace, and there will be at least one tail-walker -- a volunteer that walks the slowest across the entire 5km to ensure no one gets left behind and that the run doesn't finish till everyone finishes. These tail-walkers can sometimes be hard-core runners who finish their race in under 20 minutes, but instead choose to walk today to ensure an old aunty or uncle doing their 1 hour walk still get to finish the race, and experience everything that goes along with finishing -- It also means no one will ever be in last place, because the tail walker concludes the run, and they will always be behind you.

Sometimes when there aren't enough volunteers, the folks that finish fast, the sub 20 minute runners might finish the race and volunteer to scan barcodes. 

Everyone is welcomed and cheered, and no matter what time you finish your run, you'll be met with at least one applause, cheer or gentle pat of the back for finishing your run. First timers might ask basic run questions just before the start of the race, and just about any participant will chime with a helpful answer. 

Even regulars keep going, for many runners of park run a 5km run is too easy -- more of a warmup, yet they still show up. Even at my 30 minute 5km, I see runners who've finished their run, cool down and now doing a reverse loop in my direction while I still haven't completed my 5km. So why are these runners who are obviously a league ahead of most everyone else in the run still here? I think there's something about being in a community that keeps folks coming. 

Nobody says RTFM here. Even though we just had the briefing, and even though I might be getting ready for my run, I will answer your question about turn 3 being left and not right. 

Because Park Run is a community first, a run second and there is no third. Here is what their website say:

> parkrun is a free, community event where you can walk, jog, run, volunteer or spectate. parkrun is 5k and takes place every Saturday morning.
> parkrun is positive, welcoming and inclusive, there is no time limit and no one finishes last. Everyone is welcome to come along.

Now.....let's contrast this to StackOverflow.

If you ask a silly question in Stackoverflow -- you'll immediately know it. 

In fact, your question won't even become public until a moderator "moderates" it, until then it sits in question purgatory. When a moderator arrives, they usually come with a load of snark and pedantic-ness. The last time I tried to ask a question in stackoverflow, it was followed by 5 comments and responses from moderators before the question was eventually closed and marked as duplicate -- even though it wasn't, and I was still confused.

To the stackoverflow moderators, my confusion was my fault, and Stackoverflow wasn't here to fix my confusion. Instead it was here to ensure that only questions of the highest calibre and precision get posted. The entire experience left such a sour note that I decided right then I would no longer use it. Asking questions is tough, and if I could craft such a high precision question that would mean I already had the knowledge that I required to answer the question in the first place.

I'm not new to stackoverflow, I'm in the top 400 contributors of security stack exchange. But there's just something off about the tech community that we reduce ourselves to snarky know-it-alls instead of prioritizing the community, that somehow in this one place called "tech", the price of being nice and kind so high, it appears no one can afford it.

If Parkrun is a community first, run second. Stackoverflow is a ego-trip for moderator first, archive of answers second, business third and community never. Because everyone understands this is not the way to build a community, Stackoverflow never prioritized being a community of humans, which meant it was so fungible that ChatGPT displaced it overnight.

In Parkrun even a 15 minute runner might take the time out to make sure you don't make the wrong turn, that's the equivalent of a Google Principal SWE answering your hello-world python question. This isn't about "scaling", it's about building a community, and in a community no one should be afraid of asking a question. In stackoverflow the system eventually evolved into being almost question-phobic.

People say Stackoverflow died because of Generative AI and Coding assistants, but if Reddit can have significant growth during the same period why did Stackoverflow fail so spectacularly? There's probably more than one answer to the question, but the biggest component I suspect will be community. StackOverflow never grew into one, and therefore didn't stick with users, it was an archive, and archives can be replaced.

And so endeth Stackoverflow, to which I say good fucking riddance.
