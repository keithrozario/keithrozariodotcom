I sometimes wonder if my ego will ever allow me to report to someone younger, or less capable than me (or at least less capable in my personal assessment). I see many colleagues of mine (both past and present), descend into a pit of unhappiness because they have direct bosses who they deem unworthy based on an assessment, and the cause of this despair can be quiet revealing.

We often have a view of a world, that we are of a certain capability, and those above us in the corporate hierarchy need to be... better! Worse still there is an ego trip, that if someone above us on the ladder is perhaps not as good as we deem, than either the system is rigged, or we must somehow be worse than we imagined. Both of those imagined scenarios lead to cynicism and generally unhappiness.

In any organization large enough, you're bound to come across such a scenario. There is no perfect organization, and so in any career that last more than a handful of years, you're bound to end up in this scenario.

But....

Maybe there's another way to look at it.

I run duathlons quite often, they're triathlons without the swim. Usually a run, followed by a bike ride, followed by another run. I enjoy it, though I'm never in any danger of actually winning the event. 

I'm a bad runner -- so I often get overtaken at the run. And sometimes by people who don't "look the part". Either some petite 5 foot women, or even women who look 20 years older than me. Sometimes you see a guy whose BMI must be in the triple digits, overtake you, and you think that something must be wrong.

But the run is fair, everybody is literally carrying their own weight.

There is no explaining this away. The truth is told so plainly that there is no room for doubt. They're better runners than you -- or at the very least, they're better runners than you in this moment. But this moment of humiliation is not a cause for despair, rather it's a moment to celebrate and learn, humiliation is after all God's way of teaching you humility.

> “I began to see what the great teachers of religion meant when they said that humility is the greatest of virtues,” she later wrote, “and if you can’t learn it, God will teach it to you by humiliation. Only so can a man be really great, and it was in those accommodations to necessity that Franklin Roosevelt began to approach the stature of humility and inner integrity which made him truly great.” 
> David Brooks - Road to Character

The best way to approach a race, is by running it on your own terms. Go in with a timing in mind, based on your ability, and try to beat yourself. No one else. It's a race, but you're not racing someone with decades more experience than you, or was blessed by better genes. You're not racing someone whose putting in 100's of kilometers a week, you're racing yourself. That's the focus -- that's the discipline.

If you get distracted and try to run with someone whose truly out of your league, you'll end up far worse off. Run your own race, and leave the competitors to do their thing.

So maybe one of classmates is a Managing Director, and you're still nowhere, so what? What was your plan and are you on target? If you didn't have a plan, then that's the problem.
